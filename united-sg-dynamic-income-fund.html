Why Singapore

You spend in Singapore dollars. You should be paid in them.

Most global income funds pay in US dollars. That means every month your income is set partly by an exchange rate you have no view on and no control over — and over a decade, currency can quietly take a meaningful bite out of what you actually receive.

This fund invests in Singapore assets and pays in Singapore dollars. What is declared is what arrives.

No currency drag on your income

SGD assets, SGD distributions, no conversion between the fund's returns and your bank account.

Assets you already understand

Singapore banks, REITs, government and corporate bonds, T-bills. This is not an exotic strategy dressed as an income product.

A market built for income

Singapore's listed market has long been weighted towards dividend-paying financials and REITs rather than non-paying growth — a structural advantage for an income mandate. TODO: support with a citable market-level yield statistic and date, or delete this sentence.

Someone is actually watching this

The allocation is reviewed and rebalanced quarterly, combining UOBAM's fundamental research with machine-learning analysis of macro, valuation and technical signals. The intent is straightforward: hold more risk when it is being paid for, and step back to money market and short-duration assets when it is not.

Bottom-up security selection

Individual holdings are chosen by portfolio managers who cover these companies directly, not by index inclusion.

AI-augmented, not AI-run

Machine-learning models inform the asset allocation view. The decisions are made by people who are accountable for them.

Quarterly rebalancing

The portfolio is reset to the manager's current view four times a year, so you are not carrying last year's positioning into this year's market.

What you're signing up for

A 6% distribution is not a 6% return.

The fund aims to pay 6% a year regardless of what it earns in that year. When total return exceeds the distribution, your payout comes from income and gains and your capital is intact. When it doesn't, part of what you receive is your own money coming back, and the value of your holding falls by that much.

Over the twelve months to DD MMM 2026, the fund returned 10.15% before charges — comfortably more than the distribution. Over shorter and longer periods it has not always. That is the trade, and you should understand it before you invest rather than after.

Key risks

  • Market risk — The fund holds equities and REITs and will fall when Singapore and Asian markets fall. It declined 5.68% in the month of March 2026 alone.
  • Distributions are not guaranteed — The rate, frequency and amount are at UOBAM's discretion and can be reduced or suspended.
  • Capital may be returned as income — Where distributions exceed the fund's returns, they are paid from capital and reduce your net asset value.
  • Interest rate risk — The bond and REIT sleeves are sensitive to changes in interest rates.
  • Concentration risk — The mandate is primarily Singapore. A single small, open economy carries more concentrated exposure than a global portfolio.
  • Manager risk — The allocation is actively managed. Allocation calls can be wrong, and have been.

Frequently asked questions

How much income will I actually receive?

At the current distribution policy of 6.0% p.a. for Class A SGD Dist, an investment of S$10,000 would receive approximately S$50 a month. Distributions are not guaranteed, are set at UOBAM's discretion, and may be paid out of income, capital gains and/or capital.

Is the 6% guaranteed?

No. It is the current distribution policy, not a promise. UOBAM may vary the rate, the frequency or the amount. If the rate has been maintained without variation since launch, state that here — it is factual rather than forward-looking, and it is the most reassuring fact available.

Can the distribution come out of my own capital?

Yes. In periods where the fund's returns are lower than the distribution, part of what you receive is a return of capital, and the net asset value of your holding is reduced accordingly. This is disclosed in the prospectus and is standard for target-distribution funds. It is also why the horizon matters — over longer periods the fund's returns are what sustain the payout.

What is the difference between the Acc and Dist share classes?

Class A SGD Dist pays you a monthly distribution. Class A SGD Acc reinvests everything back into the fund instead, so your holding grows rather than paying out. Choose Dist if you want the income now, Acc if you don't need it yet.

How is this different from just buying T-bills?

T-bills give you a fixed, government-backed return and your capital back at maturity, with no income until then. This fund aims to pay you every month and to grow your capital over time, by holding equities, REITs and bonds alongside money market instruments — which means it can also fall in value. They do different jobs and many investors hold both.

How is this different from buying Singapore bank shares and a REIT ETF myself?

You can, and some people should. The fund does three things a DIY portfolio typically doesn't: it moves the allocation between five asset classes four times a year, it selects individual securities rather than tracking an index, and it produces a single monthly distribution instead of a scatter of dividend dates. You are paying a management fee for that.

What is the minimum investment?

S$1,000 for your initial investment and S$500 for subsequent investments, for Class A.

What are the fees?

Mirror section 10 once the subscription fee position is confirmed.

How often is the portfolio rebalanced?

Quarterly, based on UOBAM's asset allocation view combining fundamental research and machine-learning analysis.

I already hold Cash+ Xtra. Should I switch?

They do different jobs. Cash+ Xtra is designed for money you may need in the near term, with a focus on stability. This fund is designed for money you want to generate income from over a longer horizon, and it carries meaningfully more risk — the value of your holding will move with markets.
